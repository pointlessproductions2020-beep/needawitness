England & Wales

What does will witnessing involve?

For a standard will in England and Wales, the person making the will must sign it in the presence of two witnesses aged 18 or over, with both witnesses present at the same time. The witnesses then sign the will in the will-maker's presence.

The witnesses need to witness the act of signing — they are not being asked to approve the contents of your will. Need A Witness UK supplies independent witnesses; we do not draft, review or advise on the will itself.

Important: a witness, or the witness's spouse, should not be left anything under the will. If your circumstances are unusual or you are unsure how your will should be executed, obtain legal advice before the appointment.

Will witnessing FAQs

How many witnesses do I need for a will?

For a standard will in England and Wales, two witnesses are required. Both must be present when you sign or acknowledge your signature, and both then sign in your presence.

Can a beneficiary witness my will?

You should not use somebody who receives a gift under the will, or their spouse, as a witness. Need A Witness UK provides independent witnesses so this conflict can be avoided.

Do your witnesses need to read my will?

No. Our role is to witness the signing, not to review or approve the contents. We do not need you to upload your will through the website.

Can you tell me whether my will is legally correct?

No. We provide independent witnesses only. We do not draft wills or provide legal advice. If you are unsure whether the will itself is suitable or how it should be executed, speak to a solicitor or the professional who prepared it before booking.

Can you come to my home?

Yes. The booking system accepts a signing address and calculates mileage from our base. Other suitable locations can also be requested.

What if I need witnesses urgently?

The online calendar is designed for advance bookings. If the signing is urgent, contact us directly and we can tell you whether attendance is possible.
